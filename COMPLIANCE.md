# Sending responsibly

Leadsmith writes and sends email **from your own inbox, on your behalf**. You are the sender, so the
rules below apply to you. This is a practical summary, **not legal advice**. If you send at volume or into a
regulated market, check with a lawyer for the countries you target.

## What Leadsmith already does

- **Nothing is sent without you.** Every email needs your approval, and sending is off until you switch it on.
- **Every email says how to opt out.** Your opt-out line goes into the body, and a `List-Unsubscribe` header goes on
  every message.
- **"No" stops everything.** Replies like "unsubscribe", "remove me" or a bare "no" put the person on your
  do-not-contact list and cancel their pending emails. Bounces do the same.
- **Your postal address** (Settings → Signature) goes into every email when you fill it in. CAN-SPAM requires it for
  commercial email.
- **Humane pacing.** Business hours in the prospect's time zone, weekdays only, a warm-up limit and a gap between
  emails.
- **Strict-region flags.** Leads that look like they're in the EU/EEA, UK, Switzerland or Canada get a flag in
  Leads and Review, with that region's rule in one line. A flag doesn't block anything. It's a prompt to check.
- **Only public data.** The agents use public websites, job posts, news and free directories. They never log in to
  LinkedIn or scrape behind a login.

## Your part

| Where | The rule in one line |
|---|---|
| **United States** (CAN-SPAM) | Use an honest From line and subject, include your postal address and a working opt-out, and honour opt-outs within 10 business days. Consent isn't required. |
| **EU / EEA** (GDPR + ePrivacy) | You need a lawful basis, usually legitimate interest, for B2B. Some countries (Germany, for one) expect prior consent even for business email. If asked, say where you got their data (GDPR Art. 14), and stop as soon as anyone objects. |
| **United Kingdom** (UK GDPR + PECR) | Employees at company addresses can be emailed without prior consent if you identify yourself and offer an opt-out. Sole traders and partnerships need consent. |
| **Switzerland** (UCA) | Mass marketing email needs prior consent. Keep each email one-to-one and relevant. |
| **Canada** (CASL) | You need express or implied consent. A business address the person published publicly, with no "no unsolicited email" note, can imply consent when your email is relevant to their role. |
| **Australia** (Spam Act) | You need consent, and inferred consent can come from a conspicuously published business address. Identify yourself and include an unsubscribe. |
| **India** | No dedicated cold-email law, but the DPDP Act covers personal data. Keep it relevant and honour opt-outs. |

## Good practice everywhere

- Write to a **specific person about a specific reason** (that's what the research and the "why now" are for), not a list.
- Keep your do-not-contact list. Leadsmith checks it before every send.
- Delete data you no longer need: remove leads you've disqualified.
- Gmail's own limits and policies apply. Keep the daily limit modest. 20–40 a day per inbox is a sensible ceiling.
